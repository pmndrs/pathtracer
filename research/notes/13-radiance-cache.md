# Radiance caching for diffuse GI and reflections

Reviewed November 19, 2026 against pathtracer `dd771c8` and Denoiser `7014384`. The frame and quality numbers below come from the repository's earlier studies. The warm-up and WebNN correctness probes were run for this review.

The follow-up [execution plan](../../docs/internal/design/nrc-experiments.md) records the warm-up fix, reruns and runnable WebNN experiments. The findings below describe the reviewed revision.

## What to try first

1. Fix the inline warm-up training target. On the Mac, a synthetic path with training depth 4 and vertices through depth 6 retained the prediction from depth 6. Its target omitted the intervening light. This affects training while the warm-up gate shows a plain path-traced image.
2. Use the existing world hash as the first diffuse GI candidate. Make a missing or undertrained entry continue the path. Test a path-spread threshold and keep the first sharp reflection or refraction explicit.
3. Give the cache the shading normal and roughness actually used by the BSDF. Then compare reflectance factorization and a smaller directional MLP on textured and glossy scenes. This work benefits both portable and accelerated inference.
4. Try WebNN for batched inference with WGSL training. An NRC-shaped graph accepted changing weights and returned exact results on this Mac under the tested handoff. The frame-time question is whether that handoff, feature packing and padded batches cost less than fused WGSL.
5. Share the cache between consumers only once their definitions of indirect light agree. The raytracer's one-light hit shader and the path tracer's MIS estimator currently account for directly visible lights differently.

## Implementation review

**1. Warm-up changes the training target on long paths.** In the inline WGSL hook, every selected vertex at depth ≥ `trainDepth` writes a query index to the same `nrcSums[s0].w`. When warm, the hook continues the path for display. The radiance callback ignores indices greater than the training depth, while target construction uses the *last* saved query. With M = 4, an instrumented one-invocation dispatch of the current hook, a selected tile, and synthetic depths 0–6 produced three tail queries at 4, 5 and 6. The stored index pointed at 6. A training vertex then receives the contributions through 4 plus the throughput-weighted prediction at 6, skipping contributions at 5 and 6. Enqueue the training tail once at M, then allow the display ray to continue without touching training state. A regression should compare gated and ungated targets for identical paths/weights and check the gated beauty against plain PT. Rerun cold-start curves after the fix. [Inline hook and target construction](../../bench/nrc/cache.ts) · [Historical warm-up study](../../bench/results/NRC-PIPELINE.md)

**2. Missing cache entries can remove real indirect light.** The hook terminates at a fixed depth. Hash lookup occurs later and returns RGB zero after exhausting its coarser levels. A hit whose true radiance is zero and a miss have different meanings, but the resolve uses RGB alone. Camera cuts, moving objects and newly illuminated cells expose this. Evaluate validity in the hook or retain sufficient ray state for a continuation queue. A resolved entry from the previous update can serve a lookup while the next update is built. For adaptive termination, record the actual per-path display vertex for the training-pixel fix-up. It currently subtracts everything after one global depth. When a path never queried, subtract nothing. Keep emissive hits, NEE and MIS on the same side of the boundary in both arms. [Hash lookup and confidence threshold](../../bench/nrc/hash-grid.ts) · [Inline fix-up and resolve](../../bench/nrc/cache.ts) · [Existing task N7a](../../TASKS.md)

**3. Different glossy materials can look identical to the cache.** The hook uses an oriented geometric normal. The integrator can use a texture-perturbed shading normal. The cache rereads the base material and sets roughness to 1 whenever metalness is below 0.5. The network's final specular-reflectance input is always zero. These are reasonable simplifications for legacy Lambert/conductor Sponza. For normal-mapped metal, coated plastic or glass they discard information the BSDF uses. Keep the geometric normal for surface validity. Supply the evaluated shading frame, lobe/delta classification, roughness and reflectance for prediction. Compare predicting demodulated indirect light with the current RGB target, using identical factors for training and resolve, including zero channels. Start with Lambert, then isotropic rough conductors. A single RGB factor for a layered or transmissive BSDF needs separate validation. [Hook vertex](../../packages/pathtracer/src/integrator/outputs-wgsl.ts) · [Evaluated material and BSDF](../../packages/pathtracer/src/integrator/integrator-wgsl.ts) · [NRC encoding and targets](../../bench/nrc/cache.ts)

**4. Frozen inference still pays for training paths.** In inline mode, `encodeFrame({train:false})` skips optimizer work and target construction. The render hook still selects long training paths, emits tail queries, writes vertices and sums, and the host clears those buffers and runs the fix-up. This matters for static lighting and consumers querying an already trained cache. A render-only hook can emit display queries alone. Compare it with a frozen snapshot and identical seeds, including the diffuse/specular planes. For live training, try fewer updates when held-out error is stable and restore the budget after lighting changes. The existing code already selects whole tiles to limit divergence. [Training selection and encodeStage](../../bench/nrc/cache.ts)

**5. A GI or reflections client needs an explicit light-transport contract.** The NRC predicts the remaining outgoing radiance toward the previous vertex after local emission and MIS-weighted NEE. The remaining path can still hit a light or sky on its BSDF sample and contribute the complementary MIS term. L2 `rtShadeIndirect` accepts outgoing indirect radiance, but `rtShadeDirect` takes a full light sample without that MIS weight. Simply adding the NRC suffix there can double count part of direct illumination. Use a common estimator at the cache boundary, or train a consumer target that excludes immediately sampled direct-light hits with a matching policy for delta events. Test a diffuse receiver lit only by the sky or one area light with no interreflection, then add a colored bounce wall. A probe ray should gather emitted, direct and indirect radiance at the hit before integration into irradiance. A reflection ray should gather the same hit radiance and apply its originating BSDF throughput once. An irradiance cache applies albedo/π at a Lambert receiver. An outgoing radiance prediction already includes the hit BSDF. [Megakernel emission, NEE and hook order](../../packages/pathtracer/src/integrator/integrator-wgsl.ts) · [L2 hit shading](../../packages/raytracer/src/shade/shade-wgsl.ts) · [Existing reflections A/B](../../bench/pages/reflections-ab.ts)

Keep scene updates and cache ownership in the GI/consumer layer, along the dependency direction already chosen in the project. Expose a read-only frame snapshot to consumers. A hash can be queried inline in WGSL. For the MLP, let GI and reflection passes append hit records with consumer/output indices, evaluate one compact batch, and scatter predictions back. Have one owner produce training paths and update the snapshot. Include refresh samples in off-camera regions visible only to probes or mirrors, and test moving lights/objects for stale world-space values. [Ownership decision](../INDEX.md) · [Current benchmark-owned cache](../../bench/nrc/cache.ts)

## WebNN on this Mac

I ran an fp16 graph with five 64-wide ReLU layers and a linear output on Chrome 157.0.8103.80, headless Metal, with WebNN and explicit CoreML GPU/NPU selection enabled. Every weight matrix was a graph input. Four successive snapshots changed only the output weights. An alternating input pattern exercised row order and ReLU. The test checked every output against exactly representable half-precision values. Device names below are the requested WebNN context types. Hardware counters were not collected. [Runnable correctness probe](../probes/nrc-webnn.html)

| Batch and handoff | Observed result |
|---|---|
| 64, fp16 RGB output, GPU | Export produced a WebGPU validation error for a six-byte copy and incorrect output. The probe's requested whole-buffer copy was already four-byte aligned. |
| 64, padded RGB + zero, GPU, queued handoff | Output for requested snapshots 1, 2, 3, 4 read back as 1, 1, 2, 3 in the saved-probe run. Validation reported no error. Ordering must be checked with changing weights. |
| 64 and 4,096, padded RGB + zero, GPU and NPU, drained handoff | All four snapshots matched on every output in each configuration. After submitting the input copy and destroying the exported buffer, the control submitted an empty WebGPU batch and awaited queue completion before handing off. This establishes a working control. The minimum synchronization and its cost remain open. |
| 16,384, four channels | Both device requests failed before returning a snapshot. With the drained handoff the browser reported a dropped external instance during input upload. An earlier queued attempt failed during readback. The cause is unresolved. |

To reproduce, open the probe in a dedicated Chrome profile with `--enable-unsafe-webgpu --enable-features=WebMachineLearningNeuralNetwork,WebNNCoreMLExplicitGPUOrNPU` on this Mac. The default is 64 rows, GPU, four channels, drained handoff. Append `?rows=4096&device=npu` for the second device, `?channels=3` for the alignment control or `?handoff=queued` for the ordering control. The negative controls describe this browser build and can pass on another implementation.

**Proposed inference backend.** Leave target generation, backward pass, Adam, fp32 master weights and EMA in WGSL. Use the existing `MlpEngine.useWeights` snapshot for both displayed and training-tail queries. The denoiser builds constant convolution weights once. NRC needs mutable weight inputs or versioned snapshots. The existing weights are contiguous row-major `[out,in]`, which the probe consumes with transposed-B GEMM. Keep the true RGB loss in training and pad just the inference output. Compare packing all weights into one exported tensor and reshaping/slicing inside the graph against six independent exports. Retain the existing portable inference when WebNN interop fails or costs more. [MLP engine](../../packages/mlp/src/engine.ts) · [Denoiser WebNN runtime](../../../Denoiser/packages/denoiser-webnn/src/runtime.ts) · [WebNN dispatch and interop](https://www.w3.org/TR/webnn/)

WGSL currently fuses record encoding with inference and reads the query count on the GPU. A fixed WebNN graph needs input shaped to its batch. At 518,400 actual queries, 64 fp16 features would materialize **63.3 MiB** just for the input, before weights, outputs, and padding. The denoiser observed a 16,384-row export limit on its tested layout. At the largest batch verified here (4,096), 518,400 queries would need 127 graph dispatches if naively chunked, plus any training-tail queries. Try a wider packed external tensor reshaped inside the graph, or fewer large padded slabs at fixed resolution. Track useful queries per padded slot. Avoid a synchronous count read every frame. Include command submission boundaries, ownership transfers and any waits in **end-to-end** timing. A separate graph for backward and Adam belongs after an inference win. ## Order of experiments and decision criteria

| Order | Change and comparison | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Correct the warm-up tail. Add a closed-room long-path case and a direct-only light/sky case for the consumer boundary. Compare identical frozen weights/seeds. | Correct target at M with no duplicate tail queries, gated beauty matches plain, no excess direct energy, and diffuse/specular recomposition matches beauty including the emission bypass where enabled. |
| 2 | On the Mac, reproduce the diffuse and glossy baseline with matrix inference disabled. Compare plain, tiled fp16 MLP and hash. Then compare fixed depth against continuing on misses and a spread rule. Route mirror/delta chains to traced continuation and try the cache at a subsequent rough surface. | Lower time to the same error on diffuse GI. Preserve sharp silhouettes and glossy-region error during motion. Count actual continuations and cache misses to explain the cost. |
| 3 | Evaluate material inputs and reflectance factorization. Then compare the 64-input, five-by-64 MLP with three 32-wide hidden layers and the same 64 inputs. These shapes require 20,672 and 4,192 multiply-accumulates per query respectively. Thread the shape through cache initialization and reset. Check tiled-kernel utilization. Sweep tile selection and training steps separately. | Compare image quality and total frame cost at matched adaptation after cuts and light edits. The arithmetic ratio is an upper-bound intuition for dense work only. Current tiled kernels can spend lanes on padding. |
| 4 | Compare plain hash, low-order directional cells, and a compact MLP on glossy Sponza and on a textured, normal-mapped metal/roughness ramp beside a small bright emitter. For the prefiltered specular variant, include grazing angles and retain real ray hits for mirror/glass. | Inspect the reflection crop and diffuse-only regions at 1 and 256 frames. Report highlight width/energy, signed HDR bias, relMSE, FLIP and motion flicker. Compare after the same denoiser and upscaler as well as on raw output. |
| 5 | WebNN GPU/NPU vs tiled on Mac. On Windows compare browser portable per-record/tiled, WebNN where exposed, and the existing Dawn/Vulkan matrix kernel in its own environment. Replay identical encoded records and EMA weights through each. | Require numerical parity with the CPU/WGSL oracle and fresh weights over consecutive dispatches. Measure cold compile separately, then paired windowed p50/p95 full-frame time, packing, synchronization, inference, training and resolve. Promote only if equal-time image quality holds and the whole frame gets cheaper. |

Use the existing `nrc-check`, `nrc-study`, `nrc-pipeline-study` and `reflections-ab` harnesses. Keep the same camera path, seed sequence, reconstruction settings and lighting. Include a cold start, unseen corridor, moving light, disocclusion and an offscreen object visible only in a mirror. Use a matched Mitsuba reference where available and label any high-spp self-reference. The immediate open decisions are whether the diffuse hash wins on the Mac at equal time, which roughness/footprint warrants a real reflection continuation, and whether WebNN can hand off an entire batch fast enough.
