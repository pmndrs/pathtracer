A progressive path tracer converges over hundreds of samples. A real-time renderer gets one sample per pixel per frame and leaves the rest to a temporal denoiser and an upscaler. For that, the kernel writes more than radiance: the diffuse and specular light separated from the surface colour, normals, depth and motion.

`mode: 'realtime'` sets this up. Both schedulers support it (the wavefront without `refill` or `layers`). The [real-time frames example](https://pmndrs.github.io/pathtracer/examples/realtime/frame/) shows every plane of a live frame.

## Setup

```ts
import { PtMegakernel, ptSceneOptions, type PtLookAtCamera } from '@pmndrs/pathtracer';

const pt = await PtMegakernel.create(device, caps, ptSceneOptions(scene, { mode: 'realtime' }));
pt.setScene(scene.trace, scene.buffers);
pt.setFilm(960, 540);
```

The `realtime` preset turns on `integrator.frame`, the blue-noise sampler, reduced materials after the first bounce, ray-cone texture filtering and cheap cutout shadows. The limits start at `maxDepth` 2 without Russian roulette. Any option you set yourself wins. [Modes](/concepts/modes) lists every entry. The cutout shadows need the cutout cards tagged with an instance mask: see [Alpha cutouts](/pathtracer/integrator-options#alpha-cutouts).

The blue-noise texture ships in `@pmndrs/raytracer`: without a `blueNoise` option, `create` loads `gf-vec2-64` itself (`loadBlueNoise`, no fetch). Pass `blueNoise: await loadBlueNoise('gf-vec2-128')` for the larger one, or set `integrator.sampler` to keep another sampler.

## The frame loop

One `encode(encoder, 1)` is one frame: one sample per pixel, written over the previous frame. `encode` throws for more than one pass.

```ts
pt.setCamera(camera); // the first frame

function frame(camera: PtLookAtCamera) {
  pt.nextFrame(camera); // the old camera becomes the previous one
  const encoder = device.createCommandEncoder();
  pt.encode(encoder, 1);
  // ... your passes that read pt.frameBuffers, in the same encoder ...
  device.queue.submit([encoder.finish()]);
}
```

- `setCamera(camera)` sets the camera without a previous one. Use it for the first frame.
- `nextFrame(camera)` makes the current camera the previous one, then sets the new one. Motion vectors project each hit through both cameras.
- `setPrevCamera(camera | null)` sets the previous camera directly. After a camera cut, call `setCamera(camera)` and `setPrevCamera(camera)`, so the frame has zero motion, and reset your denoiser's history.
- `setFrameIndex(index)` sets the frame number the sampler uses. It advances by one with every `encode`; set it to replay a frame. `reset` sets it to 0.

The cameras are unjittered. The sampler jitters each pixel's camera ray inside the pixel, and it is the only jitter owner: motion vectors stay jitter-free, and a downstream upscaler must run without its own jitter.

## The frame planes

`pt.frameBuffers` returns the GPU buffers, valid until the next `encode`:

| Field | What |
| --- | --- |
| `accum` | The beauty: one `vec4f` per pixel, the total radiance of this frame's sample, `a` = 1. |
| `planes` | `PT_FRAME_PLANES` (7) planes of `width * height` `vec4f`, plane after plane. Plane `p` starts at byte `p * width * height * 16`. |
| `planesOffset` | Wavefront only: there `planes` is the same buffer as `accum`, and the planes start at this byte offset (a multiple of 256). |
| `width`, `height` | The film. |

Rows run top to bottom. `readFrame()` reads everything back to the CPU as a `PtFrame` for checks; keep it out of the frame loop.

| Plane | `PtFrame` field | `rgb` | `a` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `diffuse` | Diffuse-class radiance divided by the albedo factor | Hit distance of the first bounce ray (0 on a miss or for a specular path) |
| 1 | `specular` | Specular-class radiance divided by the specular factor | The same distance (0 on a miss or for a diffuse path) |
| 2 | `normalViewZ` | World-space unit shading normal of the primary hit (0 on a miss) | View-space depth, negative in front (0 on a miss) |
| 3 | `motionRoughness` | `rg`: motion, NDC current minus previous, +y up. `b`: perceptual roughness | View depth in the previous frame minus in this one |
| 4 | `albedo` | Albedo factor: the primary hit's albedo, at least 0.001 per channel (1 on a miss) | 1 when the primary lobe is specular-class |
| 5 | `specularFactor` | Specular factor: an approximate pre-integrated specular term, floored the same way (1 on a miss) | 1 when the primary ray hit |
| 6 | `emission` | Light that is not demodulated (below) | 0 |

The primary hit's lobe decides the class: a conductor or dielectric lobe is specular-class, Lambert and layered materials are diffuse-class. Everything the path gathers after the primary hit goes into that class's plane. The stored factors are the floored ones, so per pixel:

```
diffuse * albedo + specular * specularFactor + emission = radiance
```

A filter can work on `diffuse` and `specular`, which are smooth across textures, and multiply the factors back afterwards.

**The emission plane.** Light the primary surface does not modulate goes to plane 6: an emitter hit by the camera ray, the sky behind a miss, and (with the default `frameEmission: 'mirror'`) what a chain of smooth-metal reflections reaches. Divided by the albedo factor of a black emitter, a lamp would be a huge value that a filter smears. Add plane 6 back after the filter. `frameEmission: 'primary'` leaves out the mirror chains; `'none'` puts everything in the demodulated planes.

Motion covers camera movement only. Moving objects and the motion of reflections are not in the planes yet.

## Blue noise

`sampler: 'bluenoise'` reads a 2D blue-noise texture for the first few sample dimensions of every pixel: the jitter, and the light and BSDF samples of the first vertex (`blueNoisePairs`, default 6). The error of a single frame then sits at high frequencies, which a spatial filter removes better than white noise. The pattern shifts every frame (`blueNoiseShift`, default `'owen'`) so frames average to the right answer.

It needs the `blueNoise` create option: a 2-channel texture with power-of-two sides, `gf-vec2-64` or `gf-vec2-128` from the package's set.

## The chain to a denoiser and an upscaler

The library stops at the frame planes. A full real-time pipeline adds a temporal denoiser and an upscaler, which are separate packages (`denoiser` 2.0 and `@pmndrs/upscaler`). The [denoised real-time chain example](https://pmndrs.github.io/pathtracer/examples/realtime/chain/) runs one, with the raw frame beside the final image; its [`examples/shared/realtime-chain.ts`](https://github.com/pmndrs/pathtracer/blob/main/examples/shared/realtime-chain.ts) is a single file to copy as a starting point. Its data flow per frame:

1. **Path trace** at a reduced size (for example half the display), one `encode(encoder, 1)`.
2. **Pack.** A compute pass copies the planes into the textures the denoiser takes (`rgba16float` colour and guides, `r32float` depth), and derives device depth from view depth with the camera's projection.
3. **Denoise.** A temporal diffuse/specular filter (SVGF in the bench, from `pmndrs/denoiser`) takes plane 0 and plane 1 as its two inputs, the factors in planes 4 and 5 to remodulate, the normal, depth, roughness and motion as guides, and the hit distances. Its camera data comes from your current and previous cameras, with zero jitter.
4. **Composite.** Diffuse plus specular, then the emission plane added on top.
5. **Upscale** with [`@pmndrs/upscaler`](https://github.com/pmndrs/upscaler) in temporal mode, jitter off: colour, depth, and plane 3's motion as velocity.
6. **Tone map** to the canvas.

The denoiser and the upscaler submit their own command buffers; nothing in the chain reads back to the CPU. The temporal upscaler darkens small, very bright emitters seen directly (a lamp in view loses part of its energy); the rest of the image keeps its brightness.

ReSTIR DI and GI add better light sampling to the same loop. See [ReSTIR](/pathtracer/restir).
